Who Were the Signers

Famous Signers

Thomas Jefferson
Thomas Jefferson was the principal author of the Declaration of Independence, the document adopted by the Continental Congress on July 4, 1776, that announced the American colonies' independence from Great Britain. His powerful writing expressed ideas about natural rights and equality, making the Declaration one of the most important documents in American history.
John Adams
John Adams was a strong supporter of American independence and played a key role in persuading the Continental Congress to approve the Declaration of Independence. Although Thomas Jefferson wrote most of the document, Adams helped guide it through debate and was one of the first signers after its adoption in 1776.
Benjamin Franklin
Benjamin Franklin was one of the most respected Founding Fathers and served on the committee that drafted the Declaration of Independence. His diplomatic skill and support helped build unity among the colonies during the push for independence.
Samuel Adams
Samuel Adams was a leading advocate for colonial resistance against British rule and helped inspire the American Revolution. As a signer of the Declaration of Independence, he supported the colonies' decision to separate from Great Britain.
John Hancock
John Hancock was the president of the Second Continental Congress when the Declaration of Independence was adopted. He became famous for his large, bold signature on the document, which has become a symbol of signing one's name.
Roger Sherman
Roger Sherman was a member of the Committee of Five that helped draft the Declaration of Independence. He was one of the few individuals to sign all four major founding documents of the United States.
Robert Morris
Robert Morris was a wealthy merchant who used his financial expertise to support the American cause during the Revolution. By signing the Declaration of Independence, he publicly committed himself to the colonies' fight for freedom.
Charles Carroll
Charles Carroll of Carrollton was the only Roman Catholic signer of the Declaration of Independence. He was a strong supporter of independence and remained active in public service after the American Revolution.
