Common Stitches

Slip Knot
The slip knot is usually the first step in most crochet projects. It creates the starting loop that attaches the yarn to the crochet hook.
Chain Stitch (ch)
The chain stitch is the foundation of most crochet projects. It creates a series of loops that are used to begin rows, rounds, and patterns.
Slip Stitch (sl st)
A slip stitch is used to join stitches, move yarn to a new position, or finish a project. It is one of the shortest and least noticeable crochet stitches.
Single Crochet (sc)
Single crochet is one of the most basic and common crochet stitches. It creates a dense, sturdy fabric that is often used for amigurumi, scarves, and blankets.
Half Double Crochet (hdc)
The half double crochet stitch is slightly taller than a single crochet and works up more quickly. It creates a fabric that is both soft and durable.
Double Crochet (dc)
Double crochet is a popular stitch that produces a taller fabric with more flexibility and drape. It is commonly used in blankets, shawls, and garments.
Treble Crochet (tr)
The treble crochet stitch is taller than a double crochet and creates an open, airy texture. It is often used in decorative patterns and lacework.
Magic Ring (Magic Circle)
The magic ring is a technique used to start projects worked in the round. It allows the center opening to be pulled tightly closed, creating a neat finish.
Increase (inc)
An increase is made by working two or more stitches into the same stitch. This technique helps expand the size of a project and create shaping.
Decrease (dec)
A decrease combines two or more stitches into one. It is commonly used to shape projects such as hats, stuffed animals, and garments.
Front Loop Only (FLO)
Front loop only means working a stitch into only the front loop of the stitch below. This creates a visible ridge and adds texture to a project.
Back Loop Only (BLO)
Back loop only means inserting the hook into only the back loop of the stitch below. This technique creates stretch, texture, and decorative effects.

Starting a Project

The things I like to start my projects with are:

  • Size 4.25mm Crochet Hook
  • Weight 4 Yarn
  • Stitch Markers
  • Cozy Blankets

Your First Stitches

  1. Begin with a starting knot: Watch this video to find out more about making a starting knot
  2. Create your first chain stitch by putting your hook through you loop and pulling the yarn slack through the loop. This is called a yarn over. You have just made your first chain stitch! Repeat this until you reach a desired length
  3. In order to reinforce your chain, rotate your project and put your hook into the most recently done ch. You now want to create a slip stitch by pulling the yarn slack through the chain. Continue to yarn over with slst until you have gone back to where you started.
  4. Your next row will be rotating your project once again and creating slst all the way down the length back and forth. Once this is complete you should see two completed rows!
